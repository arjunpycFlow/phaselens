# Idea sourcing

Use this when someone asks for stock ideas ("find me undervalued stocks", "what has everyone overlooked?") rather than naming a company. Breadth first, then evidence, then the full PhaseLens checklist on each finalist.

## Contents

- Four search tracks
- From long list to finalists
- Honest framing

## Four search tracks

Run them independently so the sources don't contaminate each other. Convergence across tracks is the signal.

1. **Smart-money filings.** Cluster open-market insider buying (several insiders, personal money, recent, after a price drop) from Form 4s; new 13D filings (activists or strategic holders above 5%); new small- and mid-cap positions in quality funds' 13Fs. Discount 10b5-1 plan trades and option exercises.
2. **Fundamental inflection.** Revenue growth accelerating for three or more quarters with expanding gross margin, a recent turn to GAAP profit or positive FCF, few analysts (0–4), and a modest multiple on forward earnings.
3. **Niche research convergence.** Several independent, credible sources reaching the same name: small-cap fund letters, Value Investors Club, MicroCapClub, serious Substacks, specialist trade press. One fund family repeating itself is not convergence.
4. **Structural mispricing.** Spin-offs 3–15 months old with forced selling, post-reorganization equities, hidden assets or sum-of-the-parts gaps, forced sellers after exchange offers, and dated binary catalysts (regulatory decisions, contract awards, uplistings).

Exclude SPACs, going-concern companies, crypto-treasury vehicles and structures the investor can't easily own. Mark anything unverified "n/v".

## From long list to finalists

1. Keep only names where the cheapness has a reason that may be temporary and the business is still growing or has a dated catalyst.
2. Verify every finalist against primary filings: price and date, share count, TTM cash-flow lines, net cash or debt, the specific thesis facts, and red flags (going concern, restatements, auditor or CFO changes, lawsuits, short reports, dilution).
3. Value each with the method its phase allows (`valuation.py`, or `scenarios.py sales`, `ebitda` or `book`) and compute the bands.
4. Rank by asymmetry: upside to the bull value against downside to the bear value, weighted by evidence quality.
5. Diversify the final list across setups and check overlaps with the investor's holdings and caps.

## Honest framing

- State plainly what the bull cases imply (usually 2–3.5×, not 10×). Real multi-baggers are rare and are almost never visible in a screen.
- Separate names already in their buy zone from watchlist names.
- Size the whole group as a small sleeve (for example 5–6% of the portfolio, 1–1.5% per name, in two tranches).
- Disclose conflicts of interest and leave affected names out, or flag them.
