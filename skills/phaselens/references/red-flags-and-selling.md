# Yellow flags and when to sell

## Six yellow flags

Each is a reason to investigate, not an automatic sell. One flag: re-check the thesis. Two or three together, or one that contradicts a specific claim in the thesis: the thesis has changed.

1. **Declining market share.** A competitor growing faster on price, quality or execution means the advantage is eroding. Revenue can still rise while share falls — compare growth with the 2–3 closest competitors over 3–5 years.
2. **A metric stops being disclosed.** The most reliable of the six. Companies report what flatters them; when a figure they highlighted for years disappears, the trend in it has usually turned (subscriber counts, same-store sales, units shipped, AI bookings, viewing hours).
3. **Brand dilution.** A luxury name in discount channels, visibly cheaper product, prices rising faster than value delivered.
4. **A major acquisition.** Often a company buying growth because organic growth got hard. It distracts management, uses up the balance sheet, and can change the business you thought you owned.
5. **A surprise CEO departure,** especially without a named successor.
6. **An abrupt auditor change.** Rare and serious.

The most consequential signal of all: **the company has moved phase** since you bought it.

## The four reasons to sell that hold up

1. **The thesis changed.** The original thesis was wrong; management or culture changed; a mega-acquisition changed the business or the company is being acquired; the thesis played out with no second act; a scandal or accounting irregularity; the market shifted or a disruptor arrived; or the valuation reached a level no plausible future justifies (in PhaseLens terms: the price is above the bull-case value). **Test:** name the sentence in your thesis that is now false. If you can't, the price changed, not the thesis.
2. **Your priorities changed.** A genuinely better investment, cash needed within three years, less volatility needed, or you want to spend the money.
3. **Tax.** Tax-loss harvesting or tax-gain harvesting (mind wash-sale rules).
4. **You can't hold it calmly.** Losing sleep, losing interest, rebalancing, or one position that has grown too large. Trimming an oversized position is risk management; selling because it fell 30% this month turns a temporary decline into a permanent loss.

## Reasons that don't hold up

- The price fell.
- It's up a lot.
- It has gone nowhere for a year.
- You plan to buy it back lower.
- Someone confident said so.

## Write the thesis down at purchase

Three or four sentences: what the company does, why it wins, what has to stay true, and what would prove you wrong. Reread it before every sell decision.
